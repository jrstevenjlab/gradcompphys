# PHYS690: Computational Methods for Physics Research

Graduate project-based course materials for the Physics Department at William & Mary.

## Course Description

This course develops practical computational methods for research in physics. The emphasis is on building complete workflows: organizing projects, working with data and models, validating numerical results, quantifying uncertainty, and communicating results clearly and reproducibly.

The course is organized around computational methods rather than physics subfields. Examples and projects are chosen to transfer across research areas while keeping the focus on method selection, implementation, testing, and interpretation.

## Course Goals

- Build reproducible research workflows using Unix/Linux, Git, and Python-based scientific tools.
- Import, clean, visualize, and analyze scientific data with appropriate numerical and statistical methods.
- Fit physical models to data and interpret uncertainties, residuals, likelihoods, and model comparisons responsibly.
- Implement and validate numerical methods for differential equations, linear algebra problems, eigensystems, and spectral methods.
- Use Monte Carlo and stochastic methods for simulation, uncertainty propagation, and inference.
- Use the [W&M HPC cluster](./resources/wmhpc.md) for larger-scale calculations, including batch processing and parallel job submission.
- Communicate computational work through readable code, documentation, figures, reports, and presentations.

## Course Structure

1. **Unit 1: Research Computing Foundations**
   Unix/Linux workflows, project organization, Python environments, Git, documentation, and reproducibility.
2. **Unit 2: Data Analysis, Model Fitting, and Uncertainty**
   Data cleaning, visualization, fitting, residual analysis, covariance, resampling, likelihoods, and model comparison.
3. **Unit 3: Numerical Methods for Physical Systems**
   ODEs, PDEs, finite differences, linear algebra, eigensystems, FFTs, convergence, stability, and discretization error.
4. **Unit 4: Monte Carlo, Stochastic Methods, and Inference**
   Monte Carlo integration, stochastic simulation, uncertainty propagation, MCMC, Bayesian inference, and diagnostics.
5. **Unit 5: Capstone Project**
   A final research-style project that integrates method choice, implementation, validation, uncertainty analysis, and communication.
